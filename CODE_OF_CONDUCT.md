# Code of Conduct

Zuna is built in the open, by its maintainers and anyone who wants to help. This is how we expect everyone to treat each other while doing it, the maintainers included, and what happens when someone doesn't.

## Where it applies

Everywhere the project works on GitHub, in the [zuna-app](https://github.com/zuna-app) organization: issues, pull requests, reviews, discussions, commit messages, and anything else written in its repositories, `zuna-app/zuna`, `zuna-app/plugins` and the rest.

It doesn't cover Zuna servers. Each one is run by the people who host it, who make its rules and decide who's on it. The project runs none of them and can't read what's said on them, so it can't act on anything there: take a problem on a server to that server's admins.

## What we ask

- **Be kind, and assume good faith.** Most people who open an issue have run into something real, and most people who review a change are trying to make it better.
- **Talk about the work, not the person.** Say what's wrong with a change and why. "This tells the server who sent the message" helps; "this is stupid" doesn't.
- **Be patient with language.** Everything here is written in English, which is a second language for many of the people writing it.
- **Take no for an answer.** Some things are left out of Zuna on purpose, usually because they'd tell someone what the people using it do. Making the case once, with reasons, is welcome. Bringing it up again and again isn't.
- **Respect people's privacy.** It's what the project is for. Don't share what someone told you in private, or anything that identifies them, without asking them first.

## What isn't acceptable

Mostly common sense. Don't insult or harass people, and don't make anyone feel unwelcome for who they are. No threats, no sexual stuff, no trolling or spam. And since this is a privacy project, don't post anyone's private details, like their real name, address or the server they're on, unless they've said it's fine.

## Reporting

Write to **contact@zuna.chat**. Say what happened, where, and who was involved. Links are best, with screenshots too, since comments can be edited or deleted. You don't have to be the one it happened to.

The maintainers read every report. We keep who sent it to ourselves, and tell anyone else only what we must to act on it, with your permission. If a report is about one of the maintainers, they take no part in deciding it.

We'll answer to say we've read it, usually within a few days, and tell you what we decided.

A security problem doesn't go to this address: report it privately, as [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) says.

## What happens

The maintainers decide, by how serious it was and whether it has happened before:

1. **A word.** A private message saying what was wrong. A comment may be edited or hidden.
2. **A warning.** Saying what happens next time. A conversation may be locked.
3. **A break.** A block from the organization for a set time.
4. **A permanent block.** From the organization, for good.

Threats, harassment and publishing someone's private information can go straight to a block.

If you think we got it wrong, write to the same address and say why, and we'll look at it again.
