# Feature parity

For agents, not people. `CLAUDE.md` says how to keep this current. Keep it terse.

- **Status:** `yes`, `partial`, `no`, `n/a`. `n/a` is for what a platform can't have, or what would make no sense there: never a gap, and listed in "Not possible or pointless there". A platform with all of a row it can have is `yes`, and the notes say what's `n/a`. A row that's different by design says so in its notes.
- **Paths:** desktop and web paths start at `packages/ui/src/`, and iOS paths at `apps/ios/Zuna/`, unless they're written out in full.
- **Web** shares the desktop's interface (`@zuna/ui`), so its rows follow the desktop's unless a note says otherwise. What only the web does is in "The web app" below.
- **Android** doesn't exist yet (`docs/ROADMAP.md`). Add a column when it does.
- **Last full check:** 2026-11-01, by reading and searching the code on Windows. Nothing was run for it, and Swift can't be built on that machine, so iOS `yes` means the code is there. `docs/IOS_PLAN.md`'s phase reports record what iOS was tested with on a Mac.
- **Web check:** 2026-11-03 on macOS 27, `apps/web/test/web-check.mjs` in Chromium and WebKit: signing up, the title's count, reading from the first unread message, sending with a link preview, a picture, a voice message, a notification for a reply in another chat and reading taking it back (the browser's `Notification` stood in for), a reload, a second tab and "Use here", the narrow-window notice, the web's settings, and signing in again on a cleared browser; from 2026-11-04, the screen kept on while a camera is on a call. Firefox 159 on 2026-11-04, as `moz-firefox`: Mozilla's Firefox through WebDriver BiDi, since Playwright's own doesn't start on macOS 27 ("Could not find profile folder"). All of it passed there, with a text channel, an encrypted voice channel and its stream stats. From 2026-11-04 the check decodes the voice and video messages each browser sends with AVFoundation, as the iPhone plays them.

## Known gaps

Things one platform should have and doesn't.

- **Desktop: sharing into Zuna from other apps.** iOS has it in the share sheet (`apps/ios/ZunaShare`). On the desktop, files come in by dragging and pasting. macOS's Share menu would need a share extension built beside Electron in `apps/desktop`, handing what's shared to the running app, and Windows's share target needs the app installed with a package identity.
- **iOS: waking a phone for a call, and CallKit's screen for one coming in, haven't run on a device.** A call ringing an iPhone that isn't in use is pushed: through PushKit to a VoIP token where CallKit is allowed (`Voice/CallPushes.swift`, `rangByPush` in `Voice/VoiceModel.swift`), which goes to CallKit at once, called what the app knows, and is answered or let go once the app has caught up; or, where CallKit is left out, as an alert with Answer and Decline, worded by the extension (`ZunaNotifications/NotificationService.swift`, `previewCall` in `packages/core/src/push.ts`). A push to an app iOS kept in memory wakes its core, which had gone to sleep out of sight (`callPushed` in `CoreController.swift`), checked in `CallTests` by handing the push over as PushKit would (the `voipPush` control command). The server and the relay are tested (`TestACallWakesPhonesThroughTheirVoIPTokenOrAnAlert`, `TestRingsCallsThroughPushKitOrAsAnAlert`), and the alert's way in the simulator (`testACallRingsAPhoneThatIsntOpen`), through `simctl push`, which skips the extension. The simulator has no PushKit and its CallKit takes no call, so a VoIP push, CallKit's screen for a call coming in, its Answer, Decline, and End & Accept while in a voice channel, and the extension's wording need a device and the relay's key.
- **iOS: CallKit checked on an iPhone by hand, not all of it.** Each call goes to CallKit (`Voice/SystemCall.swift`): on the lock screen and in the Dynamic Island, muted and ended from the system's controls, ended by a phone call taking its place, kept out of the Phone app's recents, left out where the phone's region or its App Store is China's (`allowed()`, as App Review asks of apps on China's App Store), with LiveKit's engine waiting for CallKit to start the audio (3 s before LiveKit starts it anyway). 2026-11-03, iPhone 17 Pro Max on iOS 27.0.1, a Debug build in a call with the desktop app through a server and LiveKit on the LAN: the call showed in the Dynamic Island and on the lock screen and opened to the system's call screen; CallKit started the audio through `didActivate` 130 ms after the call was handed over, with no fallback; sound went both ways, also in the background; muting from the app and from the system's screen went across both ways, and the desktop heard nothing while muted; ending from the lock screen took the phone out of the channel; a phone call that came in and was declined left the call going. Unchecked: answering a phone call during one (holding is refused, so it should end), the Phone app's recents, an iPad, and China's App Store leaving CallKit out (it would need a StoreKit configuration set to China's storefront). The simulator's CallKit ends a call as soon as it has it, so `VoiceTests` only checks that a call handed to it goes on without it, and test builds leave CallKit out unless a test asks (`-ZunaSystemCall`).
- **iOS: a share of the phone's screen without its sound or most of its settings, and its capture only run in parts.** Only the picture goes: LiveKit's Swift SDK mixes a share's sound into the microphone's track (`ScreenShareCaptureOptions.appAudio`), past the server's rule on a share's sound and others' share volume, so the app asks for none (`startScreen` in `Voice/VoiceModel.swift`); a track of its own would need LiveKit to publish a second audio source. Of the desktop's settings for a share, only the most it may send (`VoiceSettingsView` in `Voice/CallParts.swift`): it always goes at the screen's own size, unless the server's limit holds it, and at the server's suggested frame rate (`shareTerms` in `Voice/VoiceModel.swift`). ScreenCaptureKit (iOS 27) has never captured: the simulator has none on any iOS, so its picker, and whether a landscape app arrives upright, are unchecked. Tests share moving bars past the picker (`-ZunaFakeScreen`). The broadcast extension (iOS 18 to 26, `apps/ios/ZunaBroadcast`) has never run either: LiveKit's socket for its frames goes in the App Group's container, whose path in the simulator is longer than a socket's address holds (`canShareScreen` in `Voice/VoiceRoom.swift`), so the button isn't there; it needs a device on iOS 18 to 26. Checked in the simulator on iOS 27.1 and 26.0: a share of moving bars, through the server's rules, reaching the desktop (`voice-run.ts`).
- **iOS: no push for what's said to you in a channel, waiting on Apple.** The server pushes channels only to a phone that hides notifications itself (`filters` in `server/internal/api/push.go`, `PushTargets` in `server/internal/store/push.go`), since only a device can tell what mentions someone, so the phone is only notified while the app runs (the core's `notify`). The rest is built: once Apple grants the filtering entitlement (`com.apple.developer.usernotifications.filtering`), add it to `ZunaNotifications` in `apps/ios/project.yml` and set `canFilter` in `Shared/PushStore.swift`, and the app registers with `filters`, the server pushes every channel message, and the extension drops what isn't for the person (`muted` from `previewPush`). Only the server's side is tested (`TestChannelsArePushedOnlyToPhonesThatHideNotificationsThemselves`): the extension's dropping needs the entitlement.
- **Linux: a native window only run with KWin.** GNOME, wlroots compositors and Hyprland unchecked. One that keeps a window's stream at one size and marks the window with `SPA_META_VideoCrop` would need that read in `src/capture/linux.rs`, which doesn't ask for it.
- **Linux: VA-API's smaller picture hasn't run.** NVENC's, the OpenGL blit in `native/dmabuf_cuda.c`, has, from the app on KDE with an RTX 5080. VA-API's `scale_vaapi` in `native/encoder.c` was only compiled with `ZUNA_CHECK_VAAPI` on Windows. Should it fail, the share goes through the browser, so a limit on size still holds. `--check-dmabuf` only checks the same size. NVENC or AMF from CPU memory can't do it at all (`Encoder::open` in `src/encoder.rs`), which also ends up in the browser.
- **Windows: Media Foundation only run on NVIDIA's encoder.** Intel's and any other GPU's encoder goes through Media Foundation (`h264_mf` in `apps/desktop/native/screenshare/native/encoder.c`, picked in `choose_adapter` in `src/capture/windows.rs`), converted to NV12 by D3D11's video processor and tagged BT.709 by `h264_metadata`. It has run on NVIDIA's own Media Foundation encoder, from the app (`VOICE_ENCODER=h264_mf node apps/desktop/test/voice-check.mjs`, a screen and a window at 60 fps, the run stopped before its later checks) and by itself, never on Intel's, nor on a laptop with two GPUs, where Media Foundation might pick the other's. AMF has never run either. Should either fail, the share goes through the browser.
- **macOS: one app's sound with a real screen unchecked.** Through Core Audio's process taps in the native program (`src/sound/macos.rs`), from macOS 14.2, on Apple silicon (Intel Macs aren't a target). A real screen through macOS's own picker together with its sound hasn't run, as `mac-sound-check.mjs` shares a canvas.
- **Web, Firefox off macOS: video messages unchecked.** Firefox records only WebM, so its video messages are H.264 from WebCodecs in MP4 (`H264Frames` in `lib/round.ts`, `lib/mp4.ts`), checked only with Firefox 159 on macOS 27. Where its WebCodecs can't encode H.264, they fall back to VP8 in WebM, which the iPhone can't play. Voice messages need no encoder, as their Opus is moved over as it is.

## Channels and voice channels

iOS shares its screen without sound (see Known gaps). Desktop and web paths are under `features/voice/` and `voice/` in `packages/ui/src/` unless written out, and iOS's under `Voice/` in `apps/ios/Zuna/`. Checked on Windows, and on Linux (Arch with KDE, the apps run as under X11 through XWayland, so that shares through the browser need no portal, and a person answering it for native ones), with `apps/desktop/test/voice-check.mjs` in Electron against a real LiveKit behind TLS. Its fake microphones speak through Windows's own speech, `say` on macOS, and eSpeak NG on Linux where it's installed, and beep otherwise, which leaves out the checks that need speech. On macOS 27 every check passes, run without Chromium's sandbox, whose audio service there can't open the speech, and with a moving canvas in place of the screen, as macOS's own picker can't be driven: capturing a real screen and sharing one window aren't checked there. Also `apps/web/test/web-check.mjs` in Chromium, which connects and encrypts but only has one person in a call. iOS's text channels: built and run on macOS 27 with Xcode 27.1, `ChannelsTests` in `apps/ios/ZunaUITests` against desktop peers. iOS's voice channels: built and run there too, `VoiceTests` against desktop peers and a real LiveKit, with a tone for the microphone and moving bars for the camera. Media with the desktop app, both ways, passes every check of `npm run test:ios:voice` (`tests/e2e/src/voice-run.ts`): speech each way, decrypted, before and after a new key, cameras each way, the desktop's shared screen on the phone, the phone's (moving bars, `-ZunaFakeScreen`) on the desktop, and an admin's mute. Test builds leave the simulator's microphone alone, which can take the Mac's audio daemon down, and send the tone through LiveKit's manual rendering, which still decodes what others send.

## One platform, by design

These aren't gaps, so don't port them without being asked.

- **Desktop only:** tray, title bar, automatic updates, the unread badge, and Electron's fuses.
- **Windows only:** the app's own notifications, drawn as Telegram's, with Reply and taken back once read. macOS and Linux use the system's, and the web the browser's, as asked.
- **Macs with Apple silicon only:** no build for Intel Macs is published, so nothing is built or chased for them, such as a share's sound.
- **Desktop and web:** the resizable sidebar, and the content security policy (sent by `zuna-web` on the web).
- **Desktop and web:** mouse gestures in a chat: dragging across messages to pick them, and a double click to reply. iOS keeps tapping to pick and swiping to reply, as asked.
- **Desktop only:** native screen capture, and push to talk from another window.
- **Desktop and web:** in a call, RNNoise, the gate and push to talk, the microphone test and choice of microphone, and the channel's chat beside the call. iOS uses Apple's voice processing and Voice Isolation, the system's route picker, and opens the chat with the call in the bar, as asked.
- **Web only:** one tab at a time with "Use here", the unread count in the tab's title, the "Zuna was updated" reload prompt, and the "Make the window bigger" notice below 860 × 540.
- **iOS only:** App lock with Face ID or passcode, the cover in the app switcher, the iPad split layout, and a voice call in the system's call controls through CallKit. A pure black background (`Theme.bg0`), a Channels tab, and a chat's bar as Telegram's, with the title in a bubble of Liquid Glass, on an iPhone, as asked.

## Not possible or pointless there

Marked `n/a`, and never gaps. Don't build them without being asked. One that becomes possible is a gap again.

- **Web:** a server without a domain, as a browser can't check a certificate by its key. `zuna://` links, as a page can only register `web+` schemes. Sharing into Zuna from other apps, as a page can only be a share target as an installed web app on Android. One app's sound with a share, as browsers only offer a tab's sound or the whole system's. Native screen capture and its settings, hiding the pointer among them, which Chromium ignores. Push to talk from another window, as a page only hears keys while it's in front.
- **iOS:** push to talk, as a phone has no key to hold. RNNoise and the gate, as Apple's voice processing does that, and the microphone test and choosing a microphone, as the system's route picker chooses. Sharing a screen natively, as a phone shares through the system's own capture. What the desktop shows on hover in a channel, as a touch screen has no hover: the bar of actions over a message, which a long press has, and the time on the left further down a run. Keeping the screen on while it shares the phone's screen from other apps, as iOS only lets the app in front keep it on.
